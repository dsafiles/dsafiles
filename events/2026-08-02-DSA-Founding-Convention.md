# DSA Founding Convention

- **Created:** 2026-08-02
- **Last reviewed:** 2026-08-04
- **Publication date:** 2026-08-04
- **Status:** publication candidate
- **Scope:** The negotiations and March 20–21, 1982 Detroit convention that joined the Democratic Socialist Organizing Committee and New American Movement to form Democratic Socialists of America.
- **Explicit exclusions:** This published record does not claim a complete delegate roll, roll-call record, asset transfer, staff allocation, or chapter-by-chapter merger history.

## Executive summary

Delegates from the Democratic Socialist Organizing Committee and New American Movement met in Detroit on March 20–21, 1982 and created Democratic Socialists of America. Contemporary *Democratic Left* material identifies the convention as DSA's birth and describes the publication's transition into the organ of the unified organization. DSA's later institutional history estimates an initial membership of about 6,000, nearly 5,000 from DSOC and roughly 1,000 from NAM.[^merger-issue][^dsa-history]

The convention concluded a multi-year negotiation, not a spontaneous consolidation. The predecessor organizations differed in size, culture, historical lineage, and strategy. The merger combined DSOC's labor and electoral-coalition orientation with NAM's grassroots, feminist, and New Left traditions while leaving important disagreements unresolved.[^oral-history]

## Road to Detroit

Merger talks began around 1977 and continued for approximately two and a half to three years. Cooperation in Democratic Agenda and anti-draft activity helped build trust. DSOC's 1980 “Eurosocialism and America” conference placed more emphasis on worker control and democratic socialism, which some NAM participants saw as narrowing programmatic differences.[^dsa-history][^oral-history]

The central disputes included Democratic Party strategy, feminism, anti-communism, authoritarianism, and the Middle East. Some NAM members feared absorption into a larger, more electorally oriented body; some DSOC leaders brought political habits formed in earlier anti-Stalinist conflicts. NAM lost activists during the debate. Merger participant Richard Healey later estimated the loss at as much as one-third, but this retrospective estimate has not been checked against contemporaneous rolls.[^oral-history]

## Convention and result

The March 1982 issue of *Democratic Left* announced the Detroit meeting and described DSA as the organization that would emerge from merger. It also indicated that local-level unification would continue after the national convention, demonstrating that institutional merger was a process rather than one instant.[^merger-issue]

Michael Harrington and Barbara Ehrenreich became founding co-chairs. Harrington represented DSOC's public leadership and coalition strategy; Ehrenreich represented an important socialist-feminist current associated with NAM. The pairing did not make them the only founders, nor did it settle every allocation of authority inside the new body.[^dsa-history][^ehrenreich]

DSA incorporated in Washington, D.C., on April 19, 1982. That corporate date matters for legal and governance history but is distinct from the March convention at which the political organization was formed.[^convention-2023]

## Unresolved questions

1. Obtain final delegate totals, roll calls, amendments, credentials reports, and the adopted merger agreement.
2. Reconstruct the allocation of seats, staff roles, assets, debts, and chapter charters.
3. Identify delegates and local organizations without converting attendance into endorsement of every outcome.
4. Compare contemporary dissent with later participant recollections.
5. Determine when every local DSOC and NAM body completed, rejected, or abandoned unification.
